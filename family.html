This is a special chapter of the month: our three adult children and four grandchildren joining us for our final week at Villa Astoria.

Most guests during the month will be adults, so this page is specifically for our immediate-family week. The goal is not to entertain the grandchildren every minute. It is to give three generations room to enjoy one another—with the villa as our easy home base for pool time, naps, simple meals, conversation and smaller-group adventures.

❤️ What would make this week feel special?

Not seven scheduled days. Just a few shared anchors surrounded by plenty of room to be together.

Perhaps one boat day that feels like a true family occasion.
One memorable island outing chosen for the group that wants to go.
One dinner and family photograph we will be glad we made time for.
And lots of ordinary Villa Astoria time: swimming, breakfast, cousins playing, grandparents reading with grandchildren, conversations on the terrace and people coming and going in smaller groups.

No one needs to participate in everything for the week to feel shared.

  1. Do not over-schedule.

A wonderful day together does not need to fill twelve hours—or include everyone doing the same thing.

EASY WITH THE GRANDCHILDREN

🏖️ Ammes + Avithos

With younger children, practical beaches can matter more than postcard fame. Ammes and Avithos make easier choices than building the day around Myrtos.

Why they may work for us: we can keep the outing short enough that tired grandchildren—and tired adults—can be back at the villa without turning the return trip into an expedition.

GOOD HEAT-DAY OPTION

🪨 Melissani + optional Drogarati

Melissani may work especially well with the grandchildren because it is genuinely unusual and relatively short. Add Drogarati only if the steps and a second cave still sound fun that day.

SPECIAL DAY TOGETHER

⛵ Our family boat day

A crewed cruise could work particularly well for our three generations because swimming, scenery, lunch and shade can happen without repeatedly loading everyone into cars.

What matters for our group: shade, easy boarding, toilet facilities, short cruising segments and a captain/crew comfortable with young children. Before booking, we will confirm that the operator provides flotation gear in the correct size and fit for each child’s current size/weight. We will also confirm the exact meeting/boarding point and any parking or transfer arrangements; the captain/operator has the final say on safe sea conditions and any route change, delay or cancellation.

🏊 Pool safety: one adult owns the job

With many adults present, it is surprisingly easy for everyone to assume somebody else is watching.

Use an explicit handoff:
“I've got the pool.”
“You've got the pool.”
“I've got it.”

The younger grandchildren should never be around the pool without active adult supervision. A crowd of adults nearby is not the same thing as one person clearly responsible.

☀️ A possible rhythm for our week

Morning: one outing while everyone is fresh.
Midday: lunch, pool, naps and shade.
Late afternoon: another swim or simple activity if people want it.
Evening: earlier dinner when that works better for the grandchildren.

We do not need an attraction every day. Time together at Villa Astoria is itself part of the week.

🧭 Together does not mean all together, all day

The adult children may want a longer hike, a winery, a late dinner, a north-island drive or simply time on their own. The grandparents and grandchildren may choose something shorter—or stay at the villa. Other days, the combinations can reverse.

The week belongs to everyone. Smaller groups are not a departure from the gathering; they are one of the ways a multigenerational gathering can work well.

👵👴 Grandparent time—and parent time

We do not need to travel as one large unit. Grandparents can take one or two grandchildren to the bakery or pool while parents explore; parents can take the children out while grandparents rest; smaller combinations can form naturally.

At some point during the week, the parents may enjoy a few hours or dinner on their own while grandparents stay with the grandchildren—and another day the grandparents can claim the afternoon off. Fair is fair.

🍽️ Meals during our family week

Greek tavernas are often naturally welcoming to children, but for us the best restaurant may simply be one where the grandchildren are comfortable, food arrives without ceremony and the adults still eat well. Earlier dinners may be everyone's friend.

A private-chef night could be especially appealing this week if we arrange one: one of the “few evenings we may share” without loading children into cars, finding parking or waiting for a table. The grandchildren can eat earlier or head toward bedtime while the adults linger. Details will be confirmed closer to the trip.

🔄 Recheck for 2027

Before Family Week, we will reconfirm the practical details that can change: whether the donkey experience is operating and appropriate for the children’s actual ages, plus current age/riding/weight rules; cave hours, access and steps; boat operator, meeting/boarding arrangements, parking or transfers, child flotation gear and weather policy; and current beach access, facilities and swimming conditions. The ideas can stay flexible until those details are current for our actual week.

❤️ The memory we are really making

The grandchildren may remember the cave or the boat. They may just as easily remember jumping into the pool with their cousins, breakfast with grandparents, staying up later than usual, or everyone laughing around the table.

That unstructured time is not what happens between the activities. It is part of why we are gathering.
